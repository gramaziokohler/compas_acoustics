    hemisphere classmethod ¶
    hemisphere(spacing_deg, radius)

    Receivers on the upper hemisphere, nominally spacing_deg apart (ISO 17497-2 section 6.2.2).

    A Fibonacci (golden-angle) spiral of ceil(2 pi / spacing^2) points; the weights are the areas of the spherical Voronoi cells of the points together with their mirror images in z = 0, which keeps the tiling symmetric.

    Colatitude of each source in degrees (0 = straight above the sample).

    		 required
    azimuth (array - like, shape(n))

    Notes

    Presets: survey is ISO 17497-2 Table 1 (colatitude 0, 30 and 60 degrees, azimuth step 60 degrees, 13 sources), and standard uses colatitudes 0..80 degrees in 10 degree steps with a 30 degree azimuth step (97 sources). The Paris weights (Kuttruff, Room Acoustics) of a ring are (sin^2(theta_hi) - sin^2(theta_lo)) / n_azimuth, with ring bounds at the midpoints between consecutive colatitudes, 0 degrees below the first ring and 90 degrees above the last.

    diffusion_coefficient ¶

    diffusion_coefficient(intensity, weights, axis=1)

    Directional diffusion coefficient of a scattered pattern (ISO 17497-2).

    Implements eq. 6 of ISO 17497-2 with receiver weights:

    .. math::

    d = \frac{\dfrac{\left(\sum_i \tilde{w}_i I_i\right)^2}
                {\sum_i \tilde{w}_i I_i^2} - 1}{n - 1},
\qquad \tilde{w}_i = \frac{w_i\, n}{\sum_j w_j}

    where :math:I_i is the intensity at receiver :math:i, :math:w_i its weight (for example the solid angle it stands for), and :math:n the number of receivers. The weights are normalised to mean 1, so equal weights reproduce the standard unweighted formula and only their ratios matter.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    intensity ndarray

    Intensity per receiver, receivers along axis: :math:|p|^2 in the far field, or the energy per steradian of the geometric method.

    		 required
    weights ndarray

    Receiver weights, one per receiver; broadcast along axis.

    		 required
    axis int

    Receiver axis. Default is 1.

    		 1

    Returns:

    Type Description
    ndarray

    Diffusion coefficient with axis removed, 1 for a uniform pattern and 0 when one receiver carries everything (with equal weights). Where the pattern is identically zero the coefficient is 0 rather than NaN.

    measure_scattering ¶

    measure_scattering(sample, method='geometric', preset=None, band_range=ISO_BAND_RANGE, air=None, name=None, **solver_options) -> ScatteringResult

    Measure the scattering and diffusion coefficients of a sample in a virtual free field (ISO 17497-2; Heimes & Vorländer 2025).

    Three methods, all on the same source grids, bands and averaging:

    • "geometric" (the default, seconds): a fast heuristic preview, not a wave solution (compas_acoustics.lab.geometric). It needs trimesh (ideally with the embreex backend), which the lab extra installs on Python ≥ 3.11 only (pip install compas_acoustics[lab]); method="kirchhoff" runs without them (Rhino's Python 3.9 included). n_rays specular rays per source, aimed at the footprint, reflect off the rigid sample and the rigid reference (mounted in a baffle at z = 0) and are binned on a receiver hemisphere of bin_deg cells. s_max per source is one minus the overlap of the two energy histograms, the lost fraction (rays still bouncing after max_bounces) counted as one more bin; the per-band s = s_max·(1 − exp(−(k h)²)) is imposed at each band's exact centre, with h = max(2 σ, |mean|) of the height difference between the sample and the reference over the footprint (a relief: twice its standard deviation; a box: its height). There is no phase and no diffraction: no rim, no cavity resonance. d comes from the cells' energy per steradian and is the same at every band, as are the polar levels; both depend on bin_deg. The result is marked generation["heuristic"] and warned about; more than 1 % lost rays for any source is warned about too, and so is a depth curve below 0.5 at the highest band while the mean s_max exceeds 0.05 (s close to 0 although the rays scatter), and so is an object whose mean height above the plane exceeds twice the spread of its relief (a closed mesh on a backing thicker than baseplate: the backing sets the depth; pass its thickness as baseplate).
    • "kirchhoff": physical optics, the correlation coefficient below.
    • "bem": the boundary element method (mesh2scattering/NumCalc, needs Python ≥ 3.11 and the lab extra), the same correlation.

    For the wave methods the correlation coefficient (Mommertz 2000; Heimes & Vorländer eq. 3) of the sample's and the reference's pressures on a receiver hemisphere of radius 10·d, at receiver spacing min(5°, 50·λ_min/d) (ISO 17497-2 §6.2.2, Heimes eq. 6), is computed per source direction and frequency, averaged over the three frequencies of each one-third-octave band (eq. 11), weighted over the sources with the Paris weights for random incidence, and averaged per octave (ISO 354 §8.3, ISO 11654).

    Every method also returns the normalised diffusion coefficient d_n = (d − d_ref)/(1 − d_ref), d from ISO 17497-2 eq. 6 on the intensities at the receivers weighted by their solid angles (|p|² for the wave methods, per frequency, then averaged per band like s), with the same random incidence and octaves as s (result.d_angle, d_bands, d_octaves).

    A batchable solver (solver.batchable: Kirchhoff) runs source batch by source batch, each batch reduced to its correlation coefficients before the next, so the pressures of all sources are never held at once (a batch holds at most 512 MB of them). The polar levels are kept for every source direction, listed in result.polar_source_indices. A Kirchhoff run estimated longer than 10 minutes is warned about before it starts (estimate_seconds): the default "standard" preset on a 0.8 m disc takes about 5 minutes on 14 cores.

    Known limitations of the BEM (2026-10-24, Heimes & Vorländer's sine surface, d = 0.8 m): below about 700 Hz NumCalc's iterative solves do not converge and those bands are wrong; such steps are listed in generation["unconverged"], warned about in result.warnings and their octaves in result.unconverged_octaves. Where it converges, the BEM's s sits 0.03–0.10 below Heimes & Vorländer's in 11 of the 12 checked one-third octaves (Λ/λ ≥ 0.5, per angle at 31.5° and 61.74°); see BemSolver.

    scattering_freefield ¶

    scattering_freefield(p_sample, p_reference, weights, axis=1)

    Correlation scattering coefficient of a sample against its flat reference.

    Implements the free-field correlation method of Mommertz (2000), written as eq. 3 in Heimes & Vorländer (2025):

    .. math::

    s = 1 - \frac{\left|\sum p_1 p_0^* w\right|^2}
             {\sum |p_1|^2 w \cdot \sum |p_0|^2 w}

    where :math:p_1 is the pressure with the sample, :math:p_0 the pressure with the reference, and the sums run over the receivers.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    p_sample ndarray

    Complex pressure of the sample, receivers along axis.

    		 required
    p_reference ndarray

    Complex pressure of the reference, same shape as p_sample.

    		 required
    weights ndarray

    Receiver weights, one per receiver; broadcast along axis.

    		 required
    axis int

    Receiver axis. Default is 1.

    		 1

    Returns:

    Type Description
    ndarray

    Scattering coefficient with axis removed. Where both patterns are identically zero the coefficient is 0 rather than NaN.
