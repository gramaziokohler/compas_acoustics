    compas_acoustics.hybrid ¶

    The DSP of compas_acoustics.Binaural and compas_acoustics.Ambisonics: image sources driving a directional FDN.

    Needs pyFDN and spharpy, installed on Python >= 3.11.

    Modules¶

    ambisonics ¶

    Ambisonic rendering of a HybridCore: paths encoded at their directions, delay lines through the output matrix.

    The order sets the number of delay lines (default_num_delays) and their projection (ambisonic_output_matrix).

    Classes¶

    Functions:¶

    ambisonic_output_matrix ¶
    ambisonic_output_matrix(sh_order: int, directions: Coordinates, *, normalization: str = 'NM', max_leakage: float = 0.05) -> ndarray

    Output matrix C mapping delay lines to ambisonic channels.

    Column i is the spherical harmonic vector of direction i, so each delay line radiates from its own direction and the late field is diffuse in expectation. Scaled by 1 / sqrt(N) so total output energy does not grow with the number of delay lines.

    normalization="NM" is N3D with W = 1 for a unit plane wave (without the 1/sqrt(4 pi)). Its basis is orthonormal, so C @ C.T of an ideal grid is a multiple of the identity and the leakage compares like with like across orders (in SN3D the diagonal falls as 1 / (2n + 1)).

    The late field is isotropic only if C @ C.T is diagonal; its off-diagonal entries are the channels' cross-correlation, which depends on how well the grid supports sh_order. The largest of them over the mean diagonal is the leakage; a grid leaking more than max_leakage raises ValueError.

    default_num_delays cached ¶
    default_num_delays(sh_order: int, minimum: int | None = None) -> int

    Smallest workable number of delay lines for an ambisonic order.

    The grid must hold exactly one point per line and support the order within the leakage limit of ambisonic_output_matrix. The counts that do are irregular (20, 23, 24, 27, 28 ... at second order), so they are searched from 4 (N + 1) ** 2, for echo density, and never below minimum (MIN_DELAYS when None): 96 lines at orders 0 to 3, 100 at fourth order.

    render_ambisonics ¶
    render_ambisonics(core: HybridCore, sh_order: int = 1, normalization: Literal['SN3D', 'N3D'] = 'SN3D') -> HybridResult

    The core as an ambisonic response: every early path encoded at its direction, every line projected through the output matrix of its direction (ambisonic_output_matrix), ACN channels at sh_order.

    Everything is computed in N3D; normalization sets the output, SN3D (AmbiX) or N3D, both with W = 1 for a unit plane wave. An order the core's grid cannot carry raises (the output matrix's leakage check).

    diffraction ¶

    Edge diffraction around obstacles and at the room's own corners, as pseudo-sources of the early field.

    The geometry is here: which edges diffract (diffraction_edges), what blocks a path (Occluders) and the wedge-frame path over an edge (wedge_path); the acoustics is in udfa and btms. The diffracting edges are every free obstacle edge and, on a closed boundary, every room edge opening wider than pi. diffraction_paths renders them to first order by the universal diffraction filter approximation (Kirsch and Ewert 2023), an object being the sum of its edges' paths (Sec. VI); the three-sided barrier of Kirsch and Ewert (2024) is defined for an infinite double edge only and is not used here. btms_paths renders first order by BTMS and, on the direct path, second order. For max_order >= 1 one enclosure reflection is allowed before or after a first-order edge.

    enclosure_edges ¶
    enclosure_edges(geometry: RoomGeometry) -> list[Edge]

    The diffracting edges of the room's own boundary.

    Every edge of the enclosure whose air side opens wider than pi: a re-entrant room corner, the corners of a room within a room (Kirsch and Ewert 2024, Sec. 3, first-order diffraction at a room corner). A seam between coplanar faces (pi) and a rectangular corner (pi / 2) have an integer wedge index nu = pi / theta_w, where the diffracted field vanishes (Kirsch and Ewert 2023, Eq. 3), and are skipped. Edges opening less than pi at a non-integer nu are left out, with ShellEdgeWarning: Edge takes theta_w in (pi, 2 pi] only. Two faces with opposite normals in one plane (a panel of zero thickness drawn into the enclosure) meet at a knife edge, theta_w = 2 pi. A piece lying on another face of the enclosure (a partition standing on an unsplit floor) is no edge, as obstacle_edges treats it.

    The edge pieces are the sides of the enclosure's faces, split at T-junctions by its own vertices as RoomGeometry splits them to check the mesh is closed, and matched by vertex index. Pieces between the same two surfaces join where they share a vertex and stay on one line. "Coplanar" and "integer" are judged in metres, to the tolerance walls are resolved to (_MERGE_TOLERANCE); "on one line", to the larger of that and the tolerance at which the mesh accepted the T-junction. Vertex noise below _MERGE_TOLERANCE changes nothing, and T-junction offsets along a face up to the mesh's own tolerance keep a corner whole. The direction from an edge into each face comes from the face's winding, which is outward and consistent (RoomGeometry checks it), so thin and non-convex faces need no tolerance. regions names the coplanar connected surface of each face, so that shared_face tells a face shared with another edge from a coplanar face elsewhere.

    directional ¶

    Direction-dependent boundary absorption on the delay line outputs.

    Wendt, van de Par and Ewert (2014, Sec. 1.2) weight "the signal of each incidence direction ... with the reflection coefficient of the room wall corresponding to the respective parallel cube side face". Kirsch, Wendt and van de Par (2023, Sec. 1.3, Eqs. 4-5) generalise it to arbitrary geometry, which holds for a mesh: rays from the receiver sample the absorption of the first face they meet, and each line takes a weighted mean,

    w_s,k = max(v_s . v_k, 0) ** beta,   beta = K / 6,
a_k   = sum_s w_s,k alpha_s / sum_s w_s,k,

    v_s the scanned directions, v_k the line's, K the number of lines (a -3 dB lobe of 90 degrees at six). The line's output filter has the magnitude sqrt(1 - a_k) per band. It sits outside the loop, so every line still decays at target.rt; the late field loses one reflection's absorption, 10 log10(mean_k (1 - a_k)) dB, left uncompensated as in both papers.

    image_sources ¶
    image_sources(room, source_index: int, mic_index: int, spec: Pair, directivity=None, damping=None, scattered=None) -> ImageSourceSet

    Surface scattering (Ewert et al. 2025, Sec. 2.1.3) splits each image per band into a specular part, prod sqrt(1 - s_i), and a scattered part, prod sqrt(s_i), over its reflections. damping is then that of the room built with ray_tracing=True, which holds the specular product, and scattered that of the same build from complementary_scattering; the scattered gain is zero for the direct sound.

    envelope ¶

    A ray-traced energy envelope imposed on the network output.

    In a room with unevenly distributed absorption the late field does not decay exponentially from the transition order: the part reaching the absorbing surfaces dies early, while the network of Wendt et al. (2014) assumes a diffuse field from its input on. This module follows the hybrid approach for non-Sabinean rooms (Vorlaender 2020, Sec. 11.9 and 16.5.5): ray tracing estimates the envelope M^2(dt, f), and a synthetic fine structure is shaped to it (Heinz 1993; Schroeder 2011). The fine structure is the network's own dense, directional output; only its per-band energy over time changes. Per band b, with E the smoothed energies of the omnidirectional signals on the package scale,

    g_b(t) = min(1, sqrt(max(E_ref,b - E_early,b, 0) / E_late,b)),

    the same on every channel; a ray is logged only after the image source order, so the reference minus the image sources is what they do not carry.

    • Smoothing: a Hann window of SMOOTH_MS, which averages out the reference's stochastic fine structure.
    • Attenuate only: the gains are capped at 1, so a hybrid quieter than its reference stays so.
    • Hold: once the reference band's energy decay curve has fallen 20 dB its gain is frozen, the ratio over the preceding SMOOTH_MS, never before the early images end (_early_end) plus that window.
    • Nine bands, 63 Hz to 16 kHz: the top band of a reconstructing bank takes everything above its lower edge, so a 16 kHz band keeps the energy above 11.3 kHz out of the 8 kHz estimate; 16 kHz takes the 8 kHz gain.

    With a directional histogram, shape_late also takes the detection sphere divided into directivity groups, each holding a histogram per frequency band (Lentz, Schroeder and Vorlaender 2007, Sec. 2.4 and Fig. 6; Schroeder 2011, Sec. 5.3.4). Schroeder averages the bands into one hit probability per group, as every Poisson event there takes one broadband HRIR; the network's lines are shaped per band anyway, so here each band keeps its own: in band b every line is scaled by sqrt(p_i / q_i), its direction's share of the rays' energy in that band over its share of the lines' (directional_shares), a per-line, per-band gain as the boundary filters of Kirsch, Wendt and van de Par (2023, Eqs. 4-5) are. The lines keep each band's total and take the rays' distribution, so a direction facing a surface that absorbs one band decays faster in that band; the 16 kHz band takes the histogram's own 16 kHz row. Shaping the lines of a feedback delay network with such shares is in neither of these sources: Schroeder steers a Poisson noise process with one probability per group, and Kirsch's gains are fixed boundary filters. Its basis is the comparison with exact image-source shoeboxes.

    The package's own choices, without a reference:

    • The bins are first pooled over neighbouring directions, since a short bin holds few detections per direction. The weights are those of Kirsch et al. (2023, Eq. 4), max(u_i . u_j, 0) ** (N / 6), who use them to map 96 scanned directions onto fewer sources and not at all with 96; pooling the N groups onto themselves is not theirs.
    • The window of SMOOTH_MS and the holds, 20 dB down for the gains and 60 dB down for the shares.
    • The ray count of the histogram (histogram_rays) applies the 1 dB that Schroeder (2011, Sec. 12.1) takes for a response's envelope to one direction's share.

    directional_shares ¶
    directional_shares(histogram, lines: ndarray, fs: float, band: int) -> ndarray

    The factor on every delay line that gives one band of the lines the rays' distribution over directions, (n, n_lines).

    histogram is a DirectionalHistogram on the lines' directions; lines (n, n_lines) are one octave band of the network's lines as heard and band the histogram's row of that band; both start at the emission.     histogram_rays ¶
    histogram_rays(volume: float, receiver_radius: float, speed_of_sound: float, directions: ndarray) -> int

    The rays at which a direction's energy over one slot has a standard deviation of SHARE_ERROR_DB.

    Vorlaender (2020, Sec. 11.3.7): the hits of a detection sphere of radius r_d within dt are Poisson distributed, N pi r_d^2 c dt / V of them on average, so the level of their energy has the standard deviation 4.34 / sqrt(hits) dB (Eq. 11.20). One of n directions holds 1 / n of the hits. The Hann window of SMOOTH_MS and smooth_over_directions weight them, and a weighted sum of Poisson counts has the error of sum(w) ** 2 / sum(w ** 2) equal ones: 2/3 of the window, and the mean of that ratio over the kernel's rows.

    smooth_over_directions ¶
    smooth_over_directions(energy: ndarray, directions: ndarray) -> ndarray

    energy (n_directions, ...) pooled over neighbouring directions, same shape.

    Direction i takes the mean of all directions weighted by max(u_i . u_j, 0) ** (N / 6), the weights of Kirsch et al. (2023, Eq. 4); pooling a grid onto itself with them is the package's choice. The rows of the kernel are normalised, so on a grid whose columns sum alike (an equal-area grid) the energy of every bin is kept to within the grid's unevenness.

    design_fdn ¶
    design_fdn(target: RoomTarget, delays: ndarray | Sequence[ndarray], *, matrix: str = 'orthogonal', dc_factor: float = 1.0, nyquist_factor: float = 0.5, rng: int = 0) -> list[tuple[DSS, dict]]

    Design one FDN per decay slope of target, with one output: the lines' omnidirectional sum.

    Each network is a (pyFDN.DSS, filters) pair as pyFDN.fdn_dss_gallery returns it, filters holding its "post_delay" attenuation and its "post_output" slope weight (None for one slope).

    delays (samples) is one array shared by every slope or one per slope. C is the W row of the ambisonic output matrix, 1 / sqrt(N) per line, which the late envelope compares with its reference; renderers project the lines themselves (render). Each slope's attenuation follows target.rt (geq_bands with dc_factor and nyquist_factor) and its feedback matrix is seeded rng + slope, also any matrix other than "orthogonal", taken from pyFDN.fdn_matrix_gallery.

    A multi-exponential decay sums independent networks with one input, each weighted per band by level_k / sqrt(sum_j level_j ** 2) as an output filter, which keeps the slopes' level ratios and one network's initial energy. A single slope has no output filter.

    directional_delays ¶
    directional_delays(spec: Pair, directions: Coordinates, *, spread: float = 0.1, rng: int | Generator = 0) -> ndarray

    Delay line lengths in samples, each the room's free path along its direction.

    The room-dimension delays of Kirsch, Poppitz and Wendt (2021) and Kirsch, Wendt and van de Par (2023), per direction and for any closed geometry: a uniform field along u meets the boundary at the rate c sum_f A_f |n_f . u| / (2 V), so line i takes 2 V / sum_f A_f |n_f . u_i|. For a shoebox that is 1 / (|u_x| / L_x + |u_y| / L_y + |u_z| / L_z); its mean over the sphere is Cauchy's 4 V / S.

    u and -u share a free path, so spread jitters each length uniformly by up to that fraction, drawn from rng, before the lengths are made coprime, lest opposite lines ring together. directions are in the receiver's frame and are rotated back to the room's before projecting.

    fdn_directions cached ¶
    fdn_directions(num_delays: int = MIN_DELAYS) -> Coordinates

    The direction each delay line radiates from, an equal-area grid of num_delays points.

    The one grid the image sources are routed onto, directional_delays reads the room along, the directional histogram bins the rays on, and the renderers project the lines from.

    render ¶
    render(builds: Sequence[tuple[DSS, dict]], drive: ndarray, *, tap: ndarray | None = None, line_eq: ndarray | None = None) -> ndarray

    Run the network with a separate input per delay line; the lines' outputs, (n, num_delays).

    drive (n_samples, num_delays) enters line i in column i (B identity, D zero). Column i of the result is what line i radiates, after line_eq and the slope weights, summed over the slopes' networks; the ambisonic late field is it times C.T.

    line_eq, an SOS bank (n_sections, 6, num_delays), filters each line on its way out, after the feedback is taken, so it colours a direction without touching the decay: the directional boundary absorption of Kirsch et al. (2023). The lines go through an identity output matrix and are filtered here, as are the networks' slope weights (post_output).

    tap, of the same shape, enters each line skipping its delay, "initially skipping the FDN delays" (Ewert et al. 2025, Sec. 3.1.2): it passes the attenuation filters f, is heard at once and recirculates, exactly f(tap) at the outputs plus A f(tap) into the lines with a static A. The scattered parts of compas_acoustics.hybrid.scattering enter there.

    scattering_weights ¶
    scattering_weights(doa: Coordinates, directions: Coordinates, opposite: bool = False) -> ndarray

    Weights of each image on the delay lines of a hemisphere, shape (M, n_lines).

    prepare ¶
    prepare(spec: Pair, iss: ImageSourceSet, target: RoomTarget, *, max_order: int, duration_s: float | None = None, num_delays: int = MIN_DELAYS, drive_gain: float | None = None, directional_absorption: bool = True, late_envelope: ndarray | None = None, directional_envelope: DirectionalHistogram | None = None, diffraction: bool | Literal['udfa', 'btms'] = False, rng: int = 0, scattering: bool = False) -> HybridCore

    The format-neutral part of the hybrid for one pair: its early paths and its delay lines.

    After Wendt, van de Par and Ewert (2014) and Kirsch et al. (2021, 2023):

    1. Images up to max_order (with diffraction, diffracted paths too) are the early field, kept path by path; higher orders in iss are ignored.
    2. An FDN with one delay line per direction of an equal-area grid of num_delays points (fdn_directions), attenuation following target.rt, each line the room's free path along its direction (directional_delays).
    3. The images of order max_order also enter the network, each spread over the lines of the hemisphere opposite its arrival by the cosine to each line (scattering_weights with opposite; Ewert et al. 2025, Sec. 3.1.2), so the first output is the next order, one free path later, off the far side: the input "must equal the total energy of the Nth-order reflections" (Wendt et al. 2014, Sec. 1.3).
    4. Each line is weighted with the reflection of the boundary in its direction (directional_absorption). The lines are kept with line_directions for the renderer.

    duration_s defaults to 1.5 times the longest target RT. rng seeds the delay jitter and the feedback matrices, rng + slope for each slope. drive_gain defaults to sqrt(num_delays). late_envelope, a package-scale omnidirectional reference, attenuates the lines per band to its late energy (Vorlaender 2020, Sec. 11.9); directional_envelope, a DirectionalHistogram on the lines' directions in room coordinates, then gives every band of them the rays' distribution in that band (shape_late); the renderer applies both (HybridCore.shaped_lines).

    diffraction="udfa" (True) adds edge diffraction (Kirsch and Ewert 2023) around obstacles and at room edges wider than pi (diffraction_edges), for the direct sound and, when max_order >= 1, one enclosure reflection before or after the edge. "btms" renders first- and second-order diffraction with the BTMS model (Svensson et al. 1999), first order with at most one wall reflection, second order between visible edges, direct paths only; it is far slower than "udfa". The late field is not affected.

    scattering (Ewert et al. 2025) needs iss.scattered; the gains are then the specular parts. The scattered part of every image of orders 1 to max_order enters "those FDN channels on the same hemisphere (initially skipping the FDN delays ...)" (Sec. 3.1.2), smeared by the allpass cascade of Eqs. 12-13 with R the mean free path and weighted by scattering_weights (unit l2 norm, as one driving image) times drive_gain, as render's tap.

    from_rir classmethod ¶ 
    from_rir(rir: ndarray, fs: float, *, n_slopes: int = 1, decay_db: float = 30.0) -> RoomTarget

    Estimate the target from a reference impulse response.

    n_slopes=1 fits one Schroeder slope from -5 dB over decay_db (30: T30, 20: T20).     udfa ¶

    Edge diffraction by the universal diffraction filter approximation (UDFA).

    Single wedges: Kirsch and Ewert (2023), "A universal filter approximation of edge diffraction for geometrical acoustics", IEEE/ACM Trans. Audio Speech Lang. Process. 31, 1636-1651. Three-sided barriers (DoubleEdgePath): Kirsch and Ewert (2024), "Filter-based first- and higher-order diffraction modeling for geometrical acoustics", Acta Acustica 8, 73, Sec. 3.3. Equation numbers are the respective paper's.

    For the barrier, two simplifications the 2024 paper allows (end of Sec. 3.3.1) are taken: beta = 1 at all angles, and no low-frequency gain compensation (its Appendix). Where the paper is silent the choice is marked "reading:". The barrier is defined for an infinite double edge and is not applied to room geometry (compas_acoustics.hybrid.diffraction).

    A wedge path from a point source over one rigid wedge carries exp(-j k d) / d * udfa_transfer(path, f) relative to a unit free field exp(-j k r) / r, d being WedgePath.d_constrained. The transfer function holds the factor 1/2 and the sign of both terms of eq. (2): on the shadow side of the incident shadow boundary its incident term tends to +1/2, on the lit side to -1/2, and the direct sound's unit step is made up exactly. Diffraction is therefore rendered on both sides of every boundary, and the geometric sound must be switched by the same geometry.
